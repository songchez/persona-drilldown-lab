# PDL Idea Concepting

## Overview

Turn synthesized signal and persona drilldown material into idea concepts. This is not a marketing-copy skill. It defines the concept itself: problem, target, core experience, MVP, assumptions, and validation.

## When to Use

- After the user chooses idea concepting.
- When persona reactions reveal a real anxiety, desire, or repeated behavior.
- When the goal is product, webapp, content system, research tool, or service concept discovery.

## Inputs

- One-line signal
- Selected persona or cross-persona synthesis
- Fears/desires/behaviors/opportunity signals
- Optional idea pattern from constants: diagnostic, calculator, scenario card, checklist, comparison map

## Output for Each Concept

- Concept title
- Source signal
- Target persona
- Problem
- Why now
- Core user experience
- Minimum feature set
- What to validate next
- Risks or weak assumptions

## Concepting Rules

- Prefer 2-3 strong concepts over many shallow ones.
- Explain why the concept follows from persona data.
- Keep investment/medical/legal topics as understanding aids, not advice.
- Do not add slogans, ads, or viral hooks unless explicitly requested.

## Common Pitfalls

1. Making a generic app idea that ignores the persona.
2. Overbuilding the MVP.
3. Confusing concept design with marketing packaging.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Each concept traces back to a signal and persona reaction.
- [ ] MVP is small enough to test.
- [ ] Weak assumptions are named.
- [ ] No unsolicited marketing copy.
